I’m sharing my Bachelor’s thesis on the long-run effects of the 1891–1892 famine in the Russian Empire.

The paper asks whether a severe historical shock can continue to shape economic decisions years later—especially when a policy change suddenly makes those decisions feasible.

Using a hand-collected panel of 60 provinces and a continuous-treatment difference-in-differences design, I find that areas hit harder by the famine experienced substantially higher migration to Siberia after the Stolypin reforms relaxed barriers to resettlement.

The results suggest that policy can activate a latent “economic memory” of past crises.

🔗 Project and paper: https://github.com/alexayos/famine-stolypin-migration
